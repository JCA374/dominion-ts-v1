The model

The teacher is a search, not a trained network. It uses the RL-v3 c2 net (413 inputs → 256 ReLU → 256 ReLU → 34 option logits + 1 win estimate, 181k weights, trained by PPO in rl-v3) three ways: to propose candidates, to play both sides in every rollout, and, with value-net leaves, to judge a position.

One question, step by step

  1. Candidates: e.g. at $6 Gold, Laboratory, Market, Silver, pass and the net's pick.
  2. Draw k = 1…K: deal the hidden cards (public sampler). For every candidate, copy that same deal, make the move, and let c2 play both players greedily until the game ends (or, with a leaf, until D more turns have been played, then ask c2's value head how likely this player is to win). Record 1 / ½ / 0 (or the value).
  3. Per candidate: mean over draws = its win estimate. Per candidate, against the net's pick: the mean and standard error of the paired difference. Pairing (the same deal for every candidate) removes most of the luck of the deal: the SE of a difference is far smaller than of either estimate.
  4. Override only if the best candidate beats the pick by more than the margin (2 points) and by more than z = 2 SEs.

What this project changes

Leaves. A full game from turn 6 is ~30 more turns of two players: most of the noise in the result comes from those far-away turns, not from the decision. Stopping after D turns and using c2's value removes that noise and costs far less; the risk is that c2's value is wrong in a way that favours some options (bias). Measured: D=4 is 5× cheaper and ranks options within a position with correlation 0.87 to full rollouts (Results, side measurements).

Successive halving. Uniform draws spend as much on a hopeless "buy Copper" as on the two real contenders. Halving keeps the budget but drops the worse half after each round (never the pick), so the final pair gets most draws.

Processes. The advisor's grader is single-threaded C. Three processes deal different draws for the same question and the answers are merged exactly (the pooled mean and paired variance of all draws; a unit test checks it against one big sample). Measured 2.4× the draws in the same second on an idle laptop.
